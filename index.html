Practice

I teach

At Guang Ming Temple in Orlando I taught Buddhist philosophy, including a class called Visualizing Buddhism that merged data visualization with the Dharma. Take something complex, make it clear enough to hold. Same instinct as the day job. RYT 200, so the line about flexibility of body and mind is a practice, not a metaphor.

Workbench

I make things with my hands

3D printers and scanners, a laser cutter, a CNC mill, Arduino, soldering, vacuum forming, sculpting, sewing. Solidworks and Rhino. I am suspicious of anyone who designs objects and has never ruined one.

The fork

I turned down the developer’s chair

Around 2000, at a dot-com called eScout, I taught myself to hand-code the web before the tools existed and learned to ssh into production. They offered me a job as a developer. I said no. I did not want to stop being the person who decides what gets built. Twenty-five years later that is still the whole job description.

The turn

I started over

Four years ago I walked away from most of it. A marriage of twenty-five years. A career. A whole identity. I became a startup CTO on the other side of it. It cost me things I am not getting back, and I would still do it. I do not say that to be inspiring. I say it because most of the people I work with are sitting on something they are scared to start.
