What I do

Judgment and a fast build, in the same person

Most people hiring for this are picking between a strategist who cannot build and a builder with no taste. I am both, and that is the whole advantage.

01

Figure out what to build first

Most projects do not fail on execution. They fail because everything looked equally important on day one. I sit with the vision, find the one thing that has to be true, and tell you what to cut. You get a scope you can actually start.

02

Build it

Not a deck about the thing. The thing. Working software, in front of real users, in weeks. Twenty years of product judgment plus AI-augmented build speed is a strange combination, and it is the point.

03

Make the complicated thing clear

Data products, decision tools, anything where someone has to understand something hard and then act on it. I invented a chart type once because the existing ones were not telling the truth. That is the instinct.

The arc

Same job. Three materials.

The medium did not change on a clean date. It changed under me while I was mid-project. That overlap is the interesting part.

Atoms · from 1995

Photography and physical product

I started at Hallmark in the photo studio, a designer who shot the photographs and built them into greeting cards. Physical products, real light, the shape of a thing in a real hand. Later I led product and design across the physical lines, standing on factory floors watching a drawing turn into an object.

Pixels · around 2000

The medium changed under me

Around 2000, at a dot-com startup called eScout, I taught myself to hand-code the web before the tools existed and learned to ssh into production. They offered me a developer's chair. I turned it down to stay the designer who builds. Back at Hallmark I moved into innovation, treating anything digital as a new material, and the patents followed. A couple of my interns dreamed up an iPhone app and we built it, a beta, a proof of concept, and pitched it to Apple in Cupertino. Then came Playfair, where the work bent hard toward data. Same job, different material.

Intelligence · 2020 to now

Building with agents

Now I build with agents. Multi-agent systems, custom models, AI avatars a patient will actually sit through. The tools are unrecognizable next to a photo studio. The person on the other end is not. Same fear, same hope, same small brave decision.

The rest of it

A few things that are also true

Practice

I teach

At Guang Ming Temple in Orlando I taught Buddhist philosophy, including a class called Visualizing Buddhism that merged data visualization with the Dharma. Take something complex, make it clear enough to hold. Same instinct as the day job. RYT 200, so the line about flexibility of body and mind is a practice, not a metaphor.

Workbench

I make things with my hands

3D printers and scanners, a laser cutter, a CNC mill, Arduino, soldering, vacuum forming, sculpting, sewing. Solidworks and Rhino. I am suspicious of anyone who designs objects and has never ruined one.

The turn

I started over

Four years ago I walked away from most of it. A marriage of twenty-five years. A career. A whole identity. I became a startup CTO on the other side of it. It cost me things I am not getting back, and I would still do it. I do not say that to be inspiring. I say it because most of the people I work with are sitting on something they are scared to start.
